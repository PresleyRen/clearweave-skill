# English editing

Use this reference with the mode, scope, and preservation contract selected in SKILL.md.
Apply only the sections relevant to the requested edit; a short message needs a short pass.
These are editorial decision rules, not a classifier or a measure of who wrote a text.

## Set the register

Follow the user's explicit instructions, then applicable publication or house conventions.
Use the supplied passage and surrounding document to infer any remaining choices.
Keep an established formal register unless the requested transformation changes it.
Treat a request for natural writing as permission to improve expression within its stated scope.
It does not supply missing facts, opinions, experience, or permission for a new argument.

Identify what the reader needs: an operation, explanation, decision, account, or reflection.
Choose language that serves that purpose. Do not turn every genre into conversation.
Retain a polite greeting or thanks when it performs a normal role in the exchange.
Remove assistant wrappers from a document only when they are outside the intended content.

## Check semantic function

Ask what a suspicious phrase contributes before changing it.
A word-list match is a candidate for review; an isolated occurrence can be entirely useful.

- Remove an opening that only announces the sentence which follows.
- Replace inflated praise with a supported description already present in the source.
- Simplify a noun-heavy phrase when the action and actor are clear.
- Keep qualifications that change scope, probability, frequency, or the strength of evidence.
- Retain a transition when it expresses a real inference, contrast, concession, or sequence.
- Preserve repetition that maintains an exact technical concept or prevents ambiguous reference.

Treat words such as robust, significant, model, framework, and evidence in their actual context.
A robust estimator and statistically significant result require their technical meanings.
Do not replace a defined term with a rotating set of near-synonyms for stylistic variety.
Check the referent of every pronoun introduced while shortening a passage.

Do not manufacture specificity. An unknown quantity remains unknown.
Words such as roughly or about require a supported estimate; they cannot create one.
Keep a source gap visible in the audit without filling it with a plausible name or number.

## Repair repeated structures

Read across sentences and paragraphs when individual sentences seem sound but the passage stalls.
Find repeated work: announcing a point, stating it, restating it, and explaining that it matters.
Remove repetition only after checking whether a restatement adds a condition or consequence.

Inspect repeated contrasts, matching section openings, predictable lists, and ornamental closers.
Keep both sides of a contrast when they distinguish alternatives the reader actually needs.
A correction to an established expectation can require a negative clause.
Preserve a real set of three items; never add or delete a member to change the count.
Use parallel structure for comparable evidence, procedures, definitions, and decision criteria.

Connect adjacent statements when the source already establishes their relationship.
Do not insert because, therefore, despite, or nevertheless unless that relationship is supported.
Replace repeated short fragments with connected syntax when it improves comprehension.
Break a long sentence when dependencies are hard to follow, keeping the scope of each modifier.
Allow sentence lengths to follow the thought. Do not target a variance score or a length recipe.

Change paragraph order only when the selected mode authorizes restructuring.
Preserve information that is distributed across a heading, list introduction, or previous paragraph.
After moving a sentence, check references such as this result, the former, and the following.

## Keep agency and evidence accurate

Use an explicit actor when the source identifies one and the distinction matters.
Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, protected, or conventionally omitted.
A method, model, sensor, or system can legitimately perform an operation.
Do not invent a researcher or team merely to give every sentence a human subject.

Keep reported claims attached to their source.
Distinguish what an organisation announced, what a witness described, and what was independently verified.
Do not convert a claim in a press release into an established finding.
Do not upgrade suggests to proves, association to causation, or one test to general performance.

When an attribution is vague, inspect the supplied references before proposing a replacement.
If support is missing, flag the specific gap; do not invent an expert, study, or consensus.
Correct or remove an underlying claim only within the requested scope and available evidence.
Preserve an unverified claim as a claim when a narrow language edit cannot resolve its support.

## Protect English conventions

Keep American or British spelling consistent with the input or explicit instructions.
When the source mixes varieties, prefer the applicable house style or its dominant editable prose.
Do not normalise quotations, proper names, titles, code, or citations to match that choice.
Keep the source convention when there is no basis for selecting another one.

Use contractions when they fit the author and medium; preserve full forms in a formal passage.
Do not add slang, profanity, fragments, deliberate mistakes, or forced informality to signal humanity.
Keep useful em dashes, semicolons, parenthetical clauses, and three-part lists.
Resolve awkward punctuation through sentence meaning, without quotas for particular characters.

Preserve minus signs, en-dash ranges, mathematical symbols, identifiers, and reference markers.
Respect the spelling and punctuation of quoted material even when editing nearby prose.
Apply an explicit typography transformation only to its authorised editable spans.
Check whether the destination expects Markdown, rich text, or plain text before changing presentation.

## Adapt to the document

| Document | Put the reader's need first | Keep accurate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical | State the operation, requirement, or observed behaviour. | Terms, identifiers, versions, conditions, error cases, units. |
| Business | State the request, decision, owner, or next step. | Commitments, prices, deadlines, dependencies, politeness. |
| News | State the event and its attributable evidence. | Who said what, dates, allegations, uncertainty, chronology. |
| Blog | Develop an observation or explanation in the author's register. | Existing experience, argument, examples, and genuine viewpoint. |

Use the academic reference for papers and research reports alongside this English guidance.
Keep method details and evidence limitations even when they make a paragraph less conversational.
A marketing benefit requires support; a more energetic voice cannot create proof or urgency.
In business requests, shortening must not turn an option into a commitment or an estimate into a deadline.
In news, preserve allegation status and attribution when moving the main point earlier.
In a blog, retain a useful aside when it explains the author's perspective or advances the account.

## Use voice samples

Extract stable expression choices: directness, density, register, rhythm, humour, and formatting.
Prefer a sample from the same medium; describe uncertainty when the available sample differs.
Use only samples supplied or otherwise authorised for the task.
Do not transplant sample facts, achievements, opinions, relationships, or remembered experiences.
Do not copy distinctive phrases merely to make the rewrite resemble the sample.
Preserve a familiar term or repeated construction when it is part of the writer's deliberate style.
Do not promote a single typo, joke, or rushed message into a permanent voice rule.

## Worked edits

These examples are invented for this reference. Their details are not facts to reuse in a user's draft.

**Remove an announcement without changing the statement.**
Before: It is worth noting that the parser supports YAML and JSON.
After: The parser supports YAML and JSON.

**Replace a noun-heavy action while preserving its actor and details.**
Before: The team carried out a review of 18 tickets on Monday.
After: The team reviewed 18 tickets on Monday.

**Keep the measurement and its scope.**
Before: It is important to note that the cache reduced median latency from 90 ms to 60 ms in this test.
After: In this test, the cache reduced median latency from 90 ms to 60 ms.

**Make a request direct without changing its dependency.**
Before: I am writing to ask whether you can approve the draft by Thursday. We need approval before we send it to the printer.
After: Can you approve the draft by Thursday? We need approval before we send it to the printer.

**Retain appropriate forms without a cosmetic edit.**
Keep: Samples were stored at −20 °C.
Keep: The parser accepts CSV, TSV, and JSON.
The passive preserves an unspecified actor; the three-item list preserves the actual supported formats.

## Finish the pass

Compare the final text with the source after applying the requested edit.
Check claims, attribution, numbers, units, dates, negation, conditions, causality, and certainty.
Check protected spans and any references affected by a move or deletion.
Check that the result fits the requested English variety, register, medium, and output format.
Remove no further material merely to demonstrate effort or lower a heuristic score.
Use the main workflow's stopping and rollback rules if preservation fails.
Report only checks actually performed; separate mechanical comparisons from semantic judgement.
Return one final version in the selected mode, without exposing an unnecessary intermediate rewrite.
